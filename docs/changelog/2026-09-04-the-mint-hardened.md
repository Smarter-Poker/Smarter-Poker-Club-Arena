# 2026-09-04 - The Mint, audited and hardened

Dan, 19:25 UTC: "I left the Mint not fully upgraded or enhanced yet ... do a full audit of it, and improve, upgrade, build, enhance and optimize everything inside of this any way you want or need to." This is the audit and what it changed. **Branch** `fix/the-mint-hardened`, migration `20260904194036_the_mint_hardened` (applied 19:40:36 UTC, probed rolled-back first, mirrored byte-exact), law test `tests/the-mint-is-hardened.law.test.ts`. The console and API half is in the World Hub (`pages/api/horses/mint.js`, the Mint tab of `pages/horses/index.js`), same day.

## What the Mint is

Four database doors and one register. `fn_ca_mint` / `fn_ca_burn` (service role or platform admin; chips to a club treasury or a union bank, diamonds to a player or the house; op-id idempotent through `ca_op_claims`), `fn_mint_chips_from_diamonds` (the product door: an owner converting diamonds at 100 chips per diamond, declared to the Mint since Phase 3.1), the opening grant a new club receives, and the restoration door. All of them feed `ca_mint_ledger`, and since 18:34 UTC the register follows the journal: a deferred constraint trigger on `chip_ledger` registers every leg out of a non-circulating store at commit, and the opening baseline makes the register's net equal the supply meter's total. Above that: `pages/api/horses/mint.js` (money.read / money.write, durable 20 writes a minute, maker-checker when `ca_operator_policy.approvals_enabled`) and the Mint tab of the Horses console.

## What the audit found, and what changed (all measured 19:30-19:50 UTC)

1. **The register could be edited or deleted.** The journal has had an append-only trigger since it was built; the register had none, so a `postgres` or `service_role` session could change an amount or remove a row silently. Now `trg_ca_mint_register_append_only`: the one permitted update is linking a row to its leg (`chip_ledger_id` NULL to a value with nothing else changed); anything else needs the maintenance door, is preserved whole in `ca_ledger_mutation_log`, and raises an incident, exactly as the journal does. Probed: an amount edit and a delete both refused with `P0403`.
2. **One leg could be registered twice.** The trigger adopts an unlinked row before writing one, but nothing structural stopped two rows from linking the same leg. `ca_mint_ledger_chip_ledger_id_key`, a unique partial index (0 duplicates, asserted before creation).
3. **The doors found their own leg by shape.** `fn_ca_mint` looked up "latest leg to this target with this amount"; two mints of the same amount to the same club in the same second could link the wrong one, and `fn_ca_burn`'s shape did not match the autoledger's row at all (its rows were only linked by the trigger's adoption path). Both doors now declare the leg with a key (`mint:<op>` / `burn:<op>`), find it by that key, and abort if the balance moved without a leg under it. Probed: mint and burn each produce exactly one register row, linked to a keyed leg; replay returns the original.
4. **There was no ceiling.** The single-operation cap was 1,000,000,000 chips, five times the supply (193.1M), and the only velocity control was a watcher that files an incident five minutes after the chips exist. `ca_mint_policy` now holds a per-operation cap and a rolling 24-hour ceiling, enforced twice: `fn_ca_mint` refuses with `amount_over_the_single_mint_cap` / `over_the_rolling_24h_issuance_ceiling` (with the cap and the 24h figure in the reply), and the constraint trigger refuses AT COMMIT for every chip mint leg from every door, so no path, present or future, can issue over it. Defaults from what the platform actually does (largest single issuance ever 100,000; 1.44M in seven days; 241,203 in the last 24h): 10,000,000 per operation and 25,000,000 per 24h for chips, 1,000,000 and 2,000,000 for diamonds (supply ~1.03M). A deliberate batch (epoch reset, mass club creation) raises the row first through `fn_ca_mint_policy_set(...)` with a reason of ten characters or more; the change is recorded in `ca_mint_policy_changes` with before and after. Probed: the door refuses over both caps; a foreign door (a declared mint by direct UPDATE) is refused at commit with the ceiling lowered and registered once with it raised.
5. **A free mint was still open.** `mint_club_chips` credits `clubs.chip_pool` with no journal declaration and no register row and was executable by `service_role`; its only caller is an unreachable legacy block in the World Hub's `mint-chips.js` (the live path there is `fn_mint_chips_from_diamonds`); 0 calls since the 2026-09-02 `pg_stat_statements` reset. Closed with `mint_club_promo` (a stub that refuses everything); registry rows `closed`, `fn_ca_money_rpc_drift()` still 0. DROP after seven days of silence, with the Phase 3.3 list.
6. **The overview lied by omission.** It reported "chips issued" from a register that, since the baseline, equals the supply meter, while the panel still said the two are not supposed to match. `fn_ca_mint_overview` now carries `reconciliation` (register net live and as of the meter's snapshot, meter total, difference, unexplained drift since the baseline, snapshot age, `balanced`), `issuance` (since the baseline and in 24h, issued and retired), `policy` (caps, 24h headroom, note), and `by_origin`. `fn_ca_mint_register_vs_supply` compares as of the snapshot, so the difference is the meter's own drift at every moment rather than a difference between snapshots for anything issued in the last hour. After apply: register 193,144,847.90 = register at meter, meter 193,144,881.42, difference 33.52 = unexplained since baseline 33.52.
7. **Every row says where it came from.** `ca_mint_ledger.origin` (generated from the op id): `operator`, `journal`, `baseline`, `diamond-mint`, `opening-grant`, `restoration`, `seed`, `deletion`. Today: chips - baseline 5 rows / 193,703,644.78, journal 12 mints / 1,200,000 and 20 burns / 2,000,000, opening-grant 2 / 200,000, restoration 319 / 41,203.12; diamonds - baseline, 483 seeds / 241,500, 233 deletions / 704,925.
8. Tidy: the trigger function was executable by browser roles (harmless for a trigger, misleading to read); revoked.

## What the console shows now (World Hub, same day)

The Mint tab opens on a reconciliation card that says in one line whether every chip is accounted for: register at the meter's snapshot against the meter, the difference, the drift since the baseline and the snapshot's age, green when the difference is the meter's own drift and nothing else. Beside it the policy: both caps, issued in the last 24h, headroom. The ledger gained an Origin column and filter, and the stale copy ("not supposed to match yet") is gone.

## Left as is, with the reason

- `fn_ca_mint_velocity_watch` (every five minutes) stays: it watches the journal for any issuance leg, including from stores the register does not enforce a ceiling on, and it is the alarm for a ceiling that was raised and forgotten. It is a watcher on top of a structural guarantee, not instead of one.
- Diamonds have a register baseline of their own (another agent's DIAMOND-ACCOUNTING work, 09-04) and seed/deletion rows written by triggers; the rolling ceiling for diamonds is enforced in `fn_ca_mint` only, because diamond issuance also happens in those triggers by design.
- `fn_mint_chips_from_diamonds` keeps its own op-id replay through `chip_transactions.metadata`; it is a product door with its own contract and the register follows it through the trigger.
