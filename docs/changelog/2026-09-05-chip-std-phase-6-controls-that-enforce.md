## 6.1 A settlement from outside the platform needs an approved adjustment

`fn_settle_tournament_obligation` now refuses a source that is not `engine.*` or a registered platform source (`ca_settle_sources`, seeded from every source the obligations have ever carried plus the DB callers read from `pg_proc`) unless it names an approved `ca_manual_adjustments` row for this event, this player wallet, in chips, at least this amount; the row is marked settled when the chips move and the obligation carries its id (`tournament_obligations.adjustment_id`). A new platform caller that forgets to register is refused with `adjustment_required`, which is the point. `fn_ca_adjustment_under_10_9` writes the agent's row: actor the chip standard's service identity, approver Dan's identity under his standing written grant (CLAUDE.md 10.9, 2026-09-02), reason at least 200 characters naming the migration, so the table's four-eyes constraint holds and says something true. This morning's 200.00 to railbirdd got its row, settled and linked. Probed rolled back: unknown source without a row refused `adjustment_required`; a row too small refused `adjustment_mismatch`; an approved row paid and was settled; a platform source paid without one; a 200-character reason enforced. Live: 40 platform settlements in the first three minutes, 0 refusals.

## 6.2 The kill switch trips itself at a thousand chips

`ca_kill_switch_policy` holds Dan's threshold (1,000 chips, the roadmap's proposal; the meters read under 7 an hour all day) per meter, armed. The hourly supply meter, the BBJ meter (per pool, two snapshots) and the escrow drift check (per run, total disagreement) each call `fn_ca_kill_switch_trip` with their figure. Crossing it opens the payout freezes named in the policy (`tournament_payouts`, and a new scope `bbj_payouts`), files a critical incident, pages the senior list, raises a financial alert, and never takes the meter down with it (its own failure is an alert, the freeze rows already written stand). While frozen, `fn_settle_tournament_obligation` refuses with `payout_frozen` and obligations stay owed; `bbj_atomic_payout_v2` refuses with a message the engine's payout queue retries, so a jackpot hit during a freeze is paid the moment it is cleared. Clearing stays human (`fn_ca_clear_payout_freeze`). Probed rolled back: 999.99 does not trip; -1,000.00 trips both scopes and files; a tournament refund and a jackpot payout are refused while frozen; a second trip in the same hour opens nothing twice. The full platform break (every table parked) is the next notch and is named, not built.

## 6.4 Every leg names its hand or its event

Read against the design before building: under the autoledger the balance write is the truth and the leg follows it inside the same statement, so a unique key on the leg that rejected a "duplicate" would leave a balance write standing with no leg, the one outcome the standard forbids (it is what the freeze did to 104 BBJ legs before 07:57). Dedupe belongs on the door, and every door is idempotent on its own op or hand id. What the ledger lacked was names. The enrich trigger now reads `app.ledger_hand_id` / `app.ledger_tournament_id`, derives the hand from a `bbj:<hand>` settlement (not from `rake:<key>`, which carries a random key when the hand is unknown, and a guessed hand is worse than none), and a spin's reserve legs carry the spin from their prize_liability side; the BBJ drop stamps `bbj:<hand>` and the hand id; the rake door stamps the hand id. Probed rolled back: a drop of 0.30 produced three legs, each naming the hand, the settings cleared after. Partitioning, the second half of 6.4, is measured (636 MB heap, 665 MB of indexes, 232k rows a day, 7M a month) and planned, not done: `ca_mint_ledger.chip_ledger_id` references `chip_ledger(id)`, and a partitioned parent's unique key must carry the partition column, so the conversion is a new table, a copy, thirteen indexes and six triggers inside one freeze window. Its own dated cut before month four (December at this volume).
