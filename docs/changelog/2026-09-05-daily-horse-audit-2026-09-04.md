## 1. The engine's nightly `daily_audit` had not completed on its own since 08-31

`fn_run_horse_daily_audit` died at the 8s service_role `statement_timeout`
(57014, at the `capture_coverage_gap` loop) and needed a session-level 180s to
finish. `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` of that one step: a parallel index scan of
`idx_hand_history_created` over 681,878 rows with a heap filter on `pot_size`,
700,896 buffers, **89.9 seconds**, returning zero rows. The function's own
`SET statement_timeout '60s'` cannot help: Postgres arms the timer when the
statement starts and a GUC change inside the call does not re-arm it, which is
why the plain call died at 8s with the 60s clause present.

Migration `20260905014049_daily_audit_fits_its_timeout_and_reads_its_own_steps`
rewrites that step: the variants dealt come from `horse_daily_nets`, the
variants reviewed from `idx_hhr_played`, and `hand_history` is probed with
`LIMIT 50` only for a variant that has nets rows and zero reviews - the
abnormal case the finding exists for.

That left 3.45s warm and **41.8s on the first cold call**: the seven-day
baseline and thirty-day first-seen scans read `horse_hand_reviews` with a
lateral unnest over `leak_tags`, every heap page for up to a month. Migration
`20260905014508_daily_audit_baseline_from_the_rollup` moves the BASELINE side
to `horse_review_rollup` (7.7 MB, 28k rows), verified first: first-seen day
identical for all 22 tags, seven-day hand and tag counts within 0.1% (the
rollup add is fire-and-forget). The day side stays exact on the reviews.

Measured after both: 3.62s, 1.52s, 3.45s warm; 9.74s once under load. Cold was
not re-measured (no way to evict the cache on production). If the 06:00 tick
still trips the ceiling on a cold day, the read it did warms the pages and the
#2628 retry ten minutes later takes the stale claim over - which the 90-second
version could never converge on.

Both migrations were applied to production with the Supabase MCP before the
files were committed, per CLAUDE.md 1.2 / `check-migrations-applied.mjs`.

## 2. A call-off all-in was counted as aggression

`HandController` writes an `all_in` with `isFullRaise` true or false when it
moved the bet and with NO `isFullRaise` when it did not - a call for everything
the player had. `HorseLogic` has read that discriminator since the V28 audit
fix. `HorseHandReview` never did: every `all_in` was a bet or raise.

Measured on 2026-09-04: **450 of 952 `river_raise_war`** tags were a hero bet
followed by a hero call-off of a shove (the `river_raise_paidoff` shape, e.g.
review 233658), and **941 of 7,569 `river_aggr_lost`** tags were hands whose
only river action by the hero was a call-off. The win-side mirrors share the
function, so `river_aggression_ev` was contaminated in both directions;
preflop, a call-off looked like a raise and suppressed `coldcall_stackoff`.

`heroActions` now carry `isFullRaise`; `isAggressiveAction` is bet, raise, or
an all-in that moved the bet; a call-off is a call wherever a detector asks.

## 3. `plo_naked_trips_stackoff` fired on sets

Three of a kind is one category number, but **275 of the tag's 419 fires were
pocket pairs on unpaired boards** (review 235357: top set on Q-J-T), and
`PLO_STACKOFF_TAGS` reads the tag as a rate into V40's `ploStackoffLoad`. The
board decides: paired board = trips = `plo_naked_trips_stackoff` (the
#5428599 shape the tag was written for); unpaired = set = new tag
`plo_set_stackoff`, measurement only, NOT in `PLO_STACKOFF_TAGS`. Whether the
set tag belongs in the V40 loop is a strategy decision for a league run.

## 4. The PLO under-full had no tag

The day's three biggest PLO losses: sixes full re-raising a river on
7-4-2-7-6 into sevens full (review 190517, -527bb); fives full of THREES -
the worst boat on 5-6-8-3-5 - raising and then shoving over a river 3-bet
(182170, -523bb); sixes full of nines 3-betting a paired turn (190109,
-519bb). Reviews 185060 and 222116 are the same shape. None carried an Omaha
tag: the V15 nut block knows flushes and straights, `nlhNutStatus.underfull`
is hold'em only, and `dominated21` excludes Omaha for boats.

New `plo_underfull_stackoff`: Omaha, showdown, 100bb+ in, full house, and
`omahaBoatIsNut` says any two unseen cards make a bigger boat or quads on this
board (exhaustive, ~800 evaluations, only for a made boat with a stack in).
NOT shipped, because it is strategy: extending `dominated21` to Omaha boats.
Recommended as a V41 flag with plo4/plo6 matchups once the tag has three days.
