## What Was There

## The Database

Migration `a_person_can_review_a_diamond_incident` (applied as `20260929211500`; the recorded text is byte-identical to the repo file):

- **The trail.** `ca_diamond_incident_events` (incident, at, kind, actor, actor_label, detail), in the shape of the chip estate's `ca_incident_events`, append-only (update, delete and truncate all refused). It has no foreign key on purpose: the daily prune deletes resolved warning and info rows after 30 days, and the trial balance watch deletes resolved info rows after 30 days, so the trail outlives the row. Every event carries the rule and severity it was about.
- **The row names its reviewer.** `ca_diamond_incidents` gained `acknowledged_at`, `acknowledged_by`, `resolved_by`, `reopened_at` and `reopened_by`. `resolved_by` is empty when a watch closed the row.
- **A person and the watches do not fight.** The health watch and the trial balance watch only ever close open rows, so a row a person resolved is never closed a second time, and a row a watch closes still records "auto: ..." with no reviewer. A trigger makes it structural: outside the review doors, nothing rewrites or re-opens a person's resolution, and a watch does not close a row a person reopened. It waits for a person.
- **Four platform-staff doors.** Staff is `fn_is_platform_admin()`, the database side of `PlatformStaffGuard`. Each door is granted to signed-in accounts and the service role, never to anonymous visitors, and refuses everyone else by name. The review and family doors also refuse a call with no person behind it.

## The Rehearsal

One rolled-back transaction on production (fixture 4.25 s), through the real doors: a player, a session with no person and the service role were refused where they should be, and anonymous visitors could not call a door. Staff read, acknowledged, commented on, resolved, reopened and resolved one row, and the trail showed all five acts in order. The board paged five rows two at a time with nothing repeated. A family act closed three rows under one act id and left the row a person had reopened and the row filed after the cut-off. With the health report stubbed for that transaction, the real, unchanged health watch kept a person's resolution, closed its own row with "auto: ...", left a row a person had reopened, and touched none of the 20 real open rows. The trail refused update, delete and truncate, and kept a row's events after the row was deleted. No real incident was reviewed or closed.
