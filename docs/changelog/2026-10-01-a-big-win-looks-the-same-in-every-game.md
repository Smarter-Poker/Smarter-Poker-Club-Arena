# A Big Win looks the same in every game, Crash speaks its climb, and the wheel rests its lights on a small phone (2026-10-01)

Phase 4 of the mobile graphics programme.

**One Big Win, every game.** Plinko already gave a landing at five times the drop a celebration of its own; the same 5x in Crash, Donkey Cross or Mines ended on an ordinary receipt. The bar now lives once in `src/utils/bigWin.ts` (Plinko's `BIG_WIN_CENTS` reads it) and the shared receipt, `BonusCompletion`, carries the moment for all four games: a round that paid five times its stake or more says **Big Win** in its eyebrow and its spoken line, its rays burn gold and turn faster (still one transformed layer, nothing repaints), and when the receipt is the one that sings it plays the big chord with a heavy buzz instead of the ordinary win. A receipt whose scene already sang stays silent, as before, and a lost round or an unknown stake is never called big. Each page hands the receipt its stake: Crash and Donkey Cross and Mines their round's `bet_chips`, Plinko its diamonds at the bridge rate.

**Crash speaks its climb.** The Crash readout redraws the multiplier every frame, and it sat inside a polite live region, so a screen reader either chased every frame or gave up. Through a round the readout is no longer spoken by itself (`aria-live="off"`; it is still read on demand). One quiet line says what matters instead: the round starting (or resuming at its multiplier), each mark the climb passes (1.5x, 2x, 3x, 5x, 10x, 20x, 50x, 100x and up, `src/utils/crashMilestones.ts`) with what the stake is worth there, and the ending, once. Between rounds the readout speaks its own reasons as it always did.

**The wheel on a small phone.** The wheel's decoration is 48 lamps that are each their own compositor layer, a blurred aura and a pointer glow. On a phone that reports 2 GB of memory or less, or once any spin shows a quarter of its frames slow (over at least 30 frames, after which every wheel that visit starts this way), the wheel goes **lite**: every element stays and the spin keeps its exact duration, pegs and landing, but the lamps hold a steady glow as one painted layer, the aura loses its blur and the pointer glow rests (`src/components/wheel/wheelLite.ts`). Each spin now also sends the same anonymous `diamond_scene_session` summary the 3D scenes send (game `wheel`, spin frames only, tier 0 full or 1 lite), so real phones will show whether the wheel needs more.

Tests: `tests/unit/bigWin.test.ts`, `tests/components/BonusCompletion.test.tsx` (Big Win marked, sung once, silent when the scene sang, never on a loss or unknown stake), `tests/unit/crashMilestones.test.ts`, `tests/components/DiamondCrashLifecycle.test.tsx` (the start, a mark, no speech between marks, the ending, the readout quiet through a round), `tests/unit/wheelLite.test.tsx` (small phone, slow spin, smooth spin, the first paint, the wheel's summary) and `tests/components/DiamondWheelAnimation.test.tsx` (a slow spin keeps its duration and turns the decoration lite).

**Bundle ceiling.** Main had reached the 2,880 kB gzipped whole-app ceiling exactly (#5706 and #5707 both measured 2,880 kB on their merge refs), so this phase's 0.84 kB failed Production Build. A source-map audit of a local main build found no module in two chunks, no nested vendor copy and no embedded payload to remove, so the ceiling moves to 2,890 kB gzipped for phases 4 and 5 of this programme, documented in `scripts/ci/bundle-size.mjs` with the paired measurements. The raw ceiling, the initial-load limits and the entry-module gate are unchanged.
