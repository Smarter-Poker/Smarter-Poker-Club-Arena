# Launch reset balance test follows the database's own clock across the week rollover

The required "Accounting transactions (PostgreSQL 17) shard 1/4" check went red at Monday 2026-10-19 00:00 UTC with no code change. Cause: `scripts/ci/test_launch_reset_balance.py` seeds its rakeback periods in the current America/Los_Angeles week, but the legacy payment guard `fn_ca_legacy_period_maturity` treats a period as closed from the UTC midnight after `period_end` (read through `statement_timestamp()`). From Monday 00:00 UTC until the Los Angeles week rolls over (07:00 UTC in daylight time, 08:00 UTC in standard time), the current-week period is already mature by the guard's clock, so the `status='paid'` probe passed the guard and was refused one step later by `launch_accounting_current_week_period_requires_qualification`, which the test did not expect. Fix (test only, no production SQL changed): the paid probe now evaluates the guard's own predicate inside the same client statement as the update, so both see the identical `statement_timestamp()`, and expects the guard refusal when the period is open and the current-week refusal when it is mature. A new probe that runs on every execution marks a next-week period, whose earning week is open by any clock, as paid and requires the legacy guard refusal, so the guard is still proven every run. Proof: on 2026-10-19 at 02:18 UTC the unchanged test failed at line 279 with the current-week refusal. The changed test passed locally against PostgreSQL 17.11 inside the same window, with the paid probe reporting `legacy_guard_window=mature` and the next-week probe reporting `legacy_guard_window=open` plus the guard refusal. Separate finding, not changed here: the production guard's UTC boundary lets a Los Angeles week period be marked paid up to 7 to 8 hours before that week actually ends.
