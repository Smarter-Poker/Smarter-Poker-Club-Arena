# Never-dealt destinations retain committed arrival custody

The final fleet scan for the Coffee Break rebuy incident found another blocked tournament: Turbo Tuesday PKO, `bc8e3d86-d970-4992-9e4d-15a2d4da3261`. Its never-dealt destination `cc98f791-c408-4262-bbc9-fc6b0efa4518` parked at 03:17 UTC on October 21. One chair held a fresh 22,000-chip entry; the other held a player moved with 22,619 chips through immutable move receipt `209c4d21-22b3-481f-9e6a-fdf8f5ed638f`. That player's original entry snapshot correctly held zero chips before the tournament started. The prior reader incorrectly required that played-stack arrival to prove an initial entry or paid rebuy.

The existing private never-dealt movement proof now also accepts the original committed move receipt, as the sealed-hand movement reader already does. It binds exactly one receipt to the tournament, player, destination chair, seat number, joined time and unchanged positive integer stack. The registration must still own that chair and stack; later funding, destination exits, any dealt-hand evidence, an incomplete roster or unexpected permits still refuse admission. The original chip and Diamond rebuy proofs remain intact. No seat, stack, wallet, settled record or historical receipt is rewritten; no repair loop or new schedule is added.

The existing required PostgreSQL qualification runs the actual previous and new helper against an anonymous mixed fresh-entry/played-arrival fixture, rejects altered custody evidence, and retains the game/currency rebuy and reentry matrix. Production acceptance requires the exact guarded migration installation/readback, recovery through the existing tournament owner, and finishing-place, prize, bounty and custody readback for the newly affected event. The earlier ten-event payout evidence and deployed engine proof remain valid for their unchanged inputs.

Required accounting CI exposed an existing fixture mismatch: its expired-window case aged a receipt by 48 hours although the captured maintained lifetime is 72 hours. The rejection fixture now derives its timestamp from the actual proof lifetime plus one second; production accounting owners and assertions are unchanged. This is the minimal delivery-blocker correction.
