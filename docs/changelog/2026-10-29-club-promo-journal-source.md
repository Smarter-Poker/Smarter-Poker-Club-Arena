# Club promo sends retain their journal source

KINGS CLUB incident e8aa01bb-dd2c-4211-a3f5-396e6678e290 measured a 770-Chip promo-wallet decrease against 400 Chips of recognized journal movement. The seven immutable `club_promo_send` legs total 770; four player sends total 270 and one agent cash-wallet send is 100. Their source label is NULL. The journal readers required the recipient label to contain `promo` to identify a club promo source, so these five legs were unkeyable. The remaining two 200-Chip sends went to agent promo wallets and were recognized. This establishes a reader/label defect, not an unpaid 370-Chip obligation.

The maintained source change sets `clubs.promo_balance` immediately after the payer's existing ledger declaration. The member journal trigger now carries the validated declared counterparty label in both directions, just like the existing autoledger trigger. All three readers recognize historical unlabeled promo sides owned by an actual club regardless of recipient wallet; explicit labels retain priority and unknown owners remain unkeyable. Exact captured function hashes and reversible edits refuse unexpected function or privilege drift. Wallets and immutable journal entries are unchanged.

Native PostgreSQL 17.11 qualification reproduced -400 before and -770 after in every reader. Actual captured payer/declaration/member trigger/autoledger functions exercised four destinations, duplicate payment refusal, reverse labels, stale declarations, invalid labels and whole-operation rollback after a journal constraint failure. Auth/permission dependencies are explicit fixture stubs, so this is not production authentication proof. Required accounting CI runs the native regression. Installation and live readback are separate pending delivery steps.
