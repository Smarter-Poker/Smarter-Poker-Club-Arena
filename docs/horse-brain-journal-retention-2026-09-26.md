# Horse decision journal retention (decision D4): the archive is a ring

Horses are players; the journal captures every decision they make so Phases 6C and 6D can be reviewed from evidence. On 2026-09-25 at 19:34:05 UTC that capture stopped at the archive's 500,000-segment quota, and after #5270 it stayed paused there, honestly, until an operator made room. This change makes the quota a ring: room is made by the writer itself, inline, on every append, and capture never waits on a person again.

## The policy

- The allocation (`HORSE_DECISION_JOURNAL_ARCHIVE_MAX_SEGMENTS` segments, at most 16 records each; `HORSE_DECISION_JOURNAL_ARCHIVE_MAX_BYTES` of compressed segment bytes; the 6 GiB catalog page ceiling) is a ring. When a batch would exceed any of them, the writer retires the oldest published segments, oldest first, one at a time, inside the same reservation transaction, until the batch fits, and then records the batch. Steady state retires one or two segments per batch.
- Only a published segment is a candidate: a row of `archive_segments`, whose file was written, re-read, hashed and indexed before its catalog row committed. A reserved batch in `archive_pending` has not been published and is never retired. When nothing published remains, the same named quota refuses and the publisher pauses on it exactly as before. That is the only way a quota can hold capture now, and `/health` says so.
- A retirement deletes the segment's index rows (by rowid range with a count check, because there is no index on `segment_sha` and building one over four million rows at open would outlast the writer's start fence; a segment indexed any other way is deleted by event identity from its own file), its catalog row and its usage in one transaction, names the file in `archive_retired`, and unlinks the file after the commit. A process that dies between the two finishes the unlink at its next open or append. Nothing runs on a timer.
- A retirement is kept even when the batch is still refused afterwards (for instance at the catalog page ceiling, which frees pages only as whole leaves empty), so every attempt starts nearer to room and a paused publisher's one-a-minute probe cannot loop over the same state. At most 1,024 segments retire in one append, so an append stays well inside the publisher's five-second progress fence; an archive further over its ring than that converges over the following appends.
- A batch that could not fit an empty archive is refused by name before anything is retired. The filesystem's free space (`archive_storage_capacity`) is not a ring quota: the archive retires within its own allocation and not to make room for whatever else filled the disk, so a full disk still pauses capture and `/health` names the filesystem as the cause.
- The read-only probe a paused publisher sends once a minute now answers room whenever the next append would retire published segments to make it, and still answers the named quota when only unpublished segments remain.
- Readers see a retired record as a missing record, never as a complete window. A reader whose snapshot named a segment that the ring retired before the file was opened gets that segment as missing rather than an error.
- The 8-day Horse-only hand retention in the database is a separate mechanism and is not touched.

## The env var

`HORSE_DECISION_JOURNAL_ARCHIVE_MAX_SEGMENTS` is the ring's length and the one knob that sizes the retained window. Default and ceiling 500,000 (the catalog is sized for 8,000,000 records). On this host that is about eight days of capture in about 10 GB, on a 75 GB disk that had 21 GB free with the archive full, so the default fits and is unchanged. Lowering it retires the excess over the following appends and keeps the newest segments; raising it past the ceiling is refused at start. `HORSE_DECISION_JOURNAL_ARCHIVE_MAX_BYTES` (default 8 GiB) bounds the same ring in bytes. No engine host configuration changes with this release.

## What /health says

The `horseJournal` section keeps every field it had and adds `segments` (retained: published plus unpublished), `maxSegments` (the ring), `publishedSegments`, `pendingSegments` (unpublished), `retiredSegments` and `retiredRecords` (since the catalog was created), `compressedBytes`, `maxBytes`, and one sentence, `capture`, built on the thread that serves `/health` from those finite fields:

- `running: the archive is a ring of 500000 segments; the oldest published segment is retired when a new one needs its room; retained=500000 published=500000 unpublished=0 retired=N`
- `not running: paused since <time> at archive_segments with no published segment left to retire (only unpublished segments remain); asks again every minute; ...`
- `not running: paused since <time> because the filesystem is out of room; the ring retires only within its own allocation and asks again every minute; ...`
- and a line each for starting, recovering, failed (with the reason), stopped, unavailable and disabled.

## How capture resumes after deploy

The engine restarts on the release, the Horse decision worker starts the publisher, the writer opens the catalog (creating the two new tables `archive_retired` and `archive_ring`, no version bump, no rewrite of existing rows), finishes any pending or retired work, and answers READY. The first append finds 500,000 segments against a ring of 500,000, retires the oldest published segment from 2026-09-17, records the batch and acknowledges it. The publisher never enters `paused`; `/health.horseJournal.mode` reads `ready`, `retiredSegments` climbs from 0 and `segments` stays at 500,000. The worker logs one line, `archive ring retired its oldest published segment so capture keeps running`, the first time it retires. Segments from 2026-09-17 to 2026-09-20 (before Phase 6A) go first; at today's rate the window then slides at about 60,000 segments a day, keeping the most recent 8.3 days.

## What remains if the archive is stuck

Not applicable: there is no off-host archive target. The "archive" is the on-host segment directory and its catalog under `/var/lib/club-arena/horse-decisions/archive`; no module ships segments to Supabase or any storage bucket, and nothing was found stuck. The only external input that can still hold capture is the host filesystem itself. If `/health.horseJournal.capture` ever reads `because the filesystem is out of room`, the archive is inside its allocation and something else filled `/`; find that, do not raise the ring. Export of segments off the host before they are retired remains open work, as `docs/horse-private-durable-journal-2026-09-14.md` already lists; until then, evidence older than the ring's window exists only in the reviews already written from it.
