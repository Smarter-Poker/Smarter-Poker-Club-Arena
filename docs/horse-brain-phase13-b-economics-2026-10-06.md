## Method

**Rigged real hands.** `JointEconomicsHarness.test-support.ts` fixes every card a real `HandController` deals: hole cards in seat order, then every flop, every turn and every river in board order, then the rest of the deck. The controller posts its own blinds, individual or big blind antes, straddle and bomb antes, decides every action's legality and turn order, returns uncalled money, builds the pots, splits the boards, takes rake and the BBJ drop and pays the winners. The harness asserts the dealt hole cards and boards are the rigged ones, and records the players exactly as `completeHandInner` receives them.

**The model on the controller's own decision.** Each case plays the rig to hero's decision, builds the decision state with the P12.1 controller-state builder (plus the bomb boards, board count and straddle flag), samples joint evidence with the real sampler, and then gives every joint sample the hand's true showdown scores, scored exactly as the sampler scores them. The samples therefore differ only in the declared response draws, which makes every line the model enumerates a line the real hand can play.

**Every terminal branch against the controller.** The test captures each terminal settlement the model makes (round 2 tree and round 1 one-response model alike) at the shared pot and deduction owners, in evaluation order. Each distinct final state is replayed on a fresh controller with the same deck: hero's candidate, then each seat driven by its final state (the one raiser is identified from the final wagers; a seat whose final wager is what it already had folds when it owes; anyone else calls or checks). The controller decides the turn order and legality of every step, and the controller's final investments and folds must equal the branch's. Then, for every branch:

1. the model's terminal payout for every seat, its rake and its BBJ drop equal the controller's;
2. the joint terminal owner (`settleJointTerminal`) run on the controller's own final state equals the controller;
3. the independent benchmark reference (`settleJointBoardReference`) gross awards and refunds bound every payout, and equal it exactly when no fee is taken; every chip paid in is paid out as a fee or to a seat;
4. the row's reachable minimum and maximum equal the extremes of the controller's settlements.

Where the one raise slot belongs to the first responder after hero, every sample's raise weight is that responder's reported raise probability, and the row's expected net chips, expected rake and expected BBJ must equal the controller's settlements of each sample's no-raise and raise lines, weighted. Each case also pins its economically distinct lines by hand.

Across the 12 controller cases this is 407 distinct controller-settled lines and 1,112 terminal comparisons.

## Defects

None reproduced. Every one of the 1,112 terminal comparisons and the Phase 7 recomputation agreed on the unmodified source, so no fix was made and `JointPotDistribution.ts`, `JointDeductions.ts`, `JointResponseTree.ts` and `JointStreetBetting.ts` are untouched.

Two observations, neither a defect in the joint owner:

- The tree's street replica accumulates binary floats in seat investments (for example 19.900000000000002 where the controller has 19.90). The pot owner converts every amount to whole units with a tolerance, so settlement is exact; the tests compare investments in whole cents.
- On a single board the controller scales winners for rake by rounding each share and taking any overshoot unit from the first winner in its merged order (`scaleWinnerUnitsForRake`, kept for single-board hands by the F2 change). In the FLO8 sixths line hero's exact share 0.9467 is paid 0.94 while the two 0.2367 shares are paid 0.24. The joint model reproduces this exactly, as it must. It is controller settlement behaviour outside Horse Brain scope and is reported here for its owner, not changed.

## Matrix rows deliberately left uncovered

- **Turn decisions with the river round, replayed on the controller.** The replay reconstructs one street's line from the terminal investments; a turn branch that continues into the river round would need the turn's closing state as well. P13-A's hand-built continuation test covers the river round's chips. Reason: harness scope, not a settlement path the river cases do not already exercise (the river round settles through the same terminal owner).
- **Shared runout prefix versus distinct suffix.** A shared prefix arises only in all-in runouts (run it twice), which have no betting decision, so the response tree never prices one, and the controller settles them on a separate run-it path. Layout coverage stays with `JointCardLayout` and `JointBoardReference`.
- **Pineapple and Short Deck at the controller with a raise branch.** Their settlement differs from NLH only in hand ranking and card occupancy, already compared against the controller in `JointDeductions` (all nine variants, one to three boards) and owned by the Phase 12 suites; no economic cross-interaction is added by a raise.
- **PLO4, PLO5 and PLO8 beyond the cases above.** Same settlement code path as PLO6 and PLO8 here; covered against the controller by `JointDeductions` and against the reference by `JointPotDistribution`.
- **Three-board hi-lo.** The three-board split is exercised in Diamond NLH and the hi-lo split in two-board PLO8; their composition adds no new code path in either owner.
- **Timed rake as an economic case.** No owning economic implementation exists; it remains a named refusal.
- **Depth boundaries.** Geometry only; preserved in `JointFixedLimitDepth` and `JointLivePolicy`.
- **Cash-only availability of Diamond NLH beyond the bomb case.** Existing named refusals stand; not extrapolated to Omaha or tournaments.
