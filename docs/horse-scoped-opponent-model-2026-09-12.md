# Session-weighted scoped opponent calculation

The new pure model reconstructs one opponent, public scope, human/horse-policy cohort and training/holdout partition from a complete qualified observation window. It hashes the selected immutable evidence and all policy parameters, deduplicates exact observation IDs, rejects conflicting identities or inconsistent sessions within one hand, and produces the same estimate after reorder, duplicate delivery or JSON restart. Another node, opponent, cohort or held-out population cannot alter the training estimate or its identity. The observer cannot model itself as an opponent.

Each hand contributes at most one unit within its dealt session; each session contributes at most one unit overall. Evidence has a seven-day half-life, with a maximum thirty-day observation window. The newest hand's decayed weight caps the entire session, so thousands of older records cannot reset its effective age. The versioned prior supplies eight session-equivalent units. An estimate requires at least 80 unique observations, 40 hands, 20 sessions and 12 effective decayed sessions. Smaller populations still return the shrunk observational estimate, explicitly marked insufficient evidence.

The five action-category probabilities include conservative simultaneous Hoeffding intervals based on bounded session evidence. These bounds assume independent sessions. Prior shrinkage cannot make them narrower than the observed-frequency bound; no evidence returns full [0,1] uncertainty. These are observed-action probabilities, not causal action values, calibrated solver distributions or authority to adjust a strategy. The future caller must supply a versioned prior whose supported actions match its public node; observations outside that support are refused.

The source is bounded to 20,000 supplied observations. Oversized or incomplete windows are refused without truncation. The window's completeness must be established by the durable reader; a boolean argument is not proof that paging or late commits were handled correctly. This operation belongs in an isolated background worker. An async callback on the main or decision worker would still block that worker and is not sufficient isolation.
