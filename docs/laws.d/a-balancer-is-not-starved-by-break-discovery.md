# server/src/tournament/aBalancerIsNotStarvedByBreakDiscovery.law.test.ts

`checkTableBalance` will not plan a single move until `tournamentBreakDiscoveryComplete` is true, and the guard is right — a balancer that has not seen every pending break can plan onto a table a break already owns. Its price was the defect: asking `fn_f06_discover_breaks` for a page of one made the claim cost a walk of the whole durable cursor twice, about 2\*(N+1) server reads for an event with N pending operations, held only in process memory and discarded by the hourly engine restart. On 2026-09-29 events 87a68e55 and cb8f2dd1 held seven and five pending operations while the process-wide elimination scheduler had 381 of 408 managers queued behind four slots (oldest wait 478 s); their durable cursors advanced once and twice in twenty-six minutes, so a claim costing 16 and 12 reads needed hours of admissions and expired every sixty minutes. Neither event ever earned it, the balancer never ran, and both fields sat one funded player to a table — 38 players on 38 tables, 48 on 41 — unable to deal a hand for hours while their level clocks reached 25 and 16 and the average stack fell under one big blind. It is a ratchet, because a balancer that never runs is what leaves a field one player to a table, which is what opens the breaks that make the claim slow to earn. This law pins that seeing every pending operation and working on one of them are separate questions: the completeness claim costs a number of server reads that does not grow with N (the same at seven pending breaks as at one, and at most two, because a wrapped page that did not fill started at ordinal zero and is already the whole non-terminal set), every discovered operation is remembered so the balancer's exclusion set is complete, and the rationing `aBreakTheSweepStartsIsFinished` established is untouched — the window that suspends the work budget is opened for exactly one operation, never a whole page and never none, and every pending operation is still reached exactly once per pass.
