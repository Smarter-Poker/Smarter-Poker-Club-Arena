# server/src/tournament/aDiamondPrizeDoesNotDivide.law.test.ts

`computePlacePrize` is the canonical payout division, and its own header calls the single division at the end "the one place a half-cent is legitimately decided". That is exactly right for a chip tournament and exactly wrong for an indivisible unit: a half Diamond is not a small imprecision to be decided, it is an amount no door in this estate accepts.

So the ladder takes the tournament's own unit, the same way the cash tables were taught to. The run-it-twice per-board slice, the multi-board settlement, the tie chop and the hi-lo split all read their table's unit rather than assuming a cent; the prize ladder was the last place in the estate that still assumed one.

The law asserts what must survive the change, in both denominations: the places sum to the pool exactly rather than to within a unit, the residual lands on the last paid place rather than a headline one, a pool too small to pay every place does not overpay, and the chip ladder is unchanged to the cent on the structures production actually runs.

It also pins the short-field rule. A pool holding fewer units than there are places pays the places it can, one unit each from the top, because every other answer pays somebody more than the player who beat them.
