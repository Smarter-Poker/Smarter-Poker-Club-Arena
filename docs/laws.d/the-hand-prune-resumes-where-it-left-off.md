# tests/the-hand-prune-resumes-where-it-left-off.law.test.ts

`sp_prune_hand_history` runs every 30 seconds with a 20 second budget, and its candidate scan is `ORDER BY created_at LIMIT 250`, so every round started at the oldest row and re-evaluated every row older than the eight-day boundary that a guard retains on purpose (a cash hand with no provenance receipt, a hand of an unsettled tournament). Measured on production 2026-10-26: "Rows Removed by Filter: 219000", 7.2 s per round to find 250 rows, 90.4% of the day running, 221 hours of database time since 2026-10-04 (7.0% of all database time), six hours behind its own boundary. Migration `20261026133101` gives the pass a cursor (`smarter_private.hand_history_prune_cursor`): the scan starts after the newest `created_at` the pass has examined, an empty round parks the cursor at the boundary, and a pass starts over within 60 minutes so a released guard is pruned within the hour. Which hands prune is unchanged.

The law pins that the installing migration is in the repo with the preimage digest it patched, unique anchors and a post-install check; that the cursor's lower bound sits beside the age bound on the same index and the round carries `max(created_at)` out; that a pass is bounded to 60 minutes and restarts from the oldest row; that an empty round parks the cursor at the boundary and a full one advances it only to rows actually examined; that the cursor is written in the same transaction as the deletes; that the owner's `horse_retention_days` config row, the guards and `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` are required to survive and the attribution DELETE never returns; that the cursor table is private to the job with no foreign key; and that no later full rewrite of the pruner drops the cursor.
