# tests/the-owner-classifier-keeps-its-operational-kinds.law.test.ts

public.fn_is_owner_operational_notification decides which of the owner
account's notifications are operational, and every layer of store-only delivery
asks it: the capture that files such a row with the Production Alerts task
instead of his inbox, the authority trigger, the push mirror and the detector.
20260928171444 taught it smarter-poker-workers' weekly settlement problem
notices and World Hub push-health's "Push Notifications Are Off", which were
reaching his inbox and his phone. A later definition copied from an older text
would send them back there, and nothing else would notice.

The rule reads the newest definition in supabase/migrations wherever its text
is written out - any case, quoted or not, at the top level, in a DO block or in
an EXECUTE string - and never one in a comment. A definition assembled at run
time (EXECUTE of a replace() over pg_get_functiondef, or a format() that injects
the name) is not seen by it; such a migration must carry its own proof. Its whole answer must stay
COALESCE(p_user = the owner account AND (...), false), so it is owner-only; it
must keep engine_break_recovered, compare the ASCII case and whitespace folded
title with "push notifications are off" under type system and with the three
problem titles under type settlement, and take the route's marker
(workers.auto-settlement and its three alertnames). No plain literal in its body
may hold a backslash, because the body is lexed in the caller's session and one
with standard_conforming_strings off reads it differently. No later migration
may drop or rename it. A redefinition that keeps the meaning in another shape
fails too; then the law changes beside it, where a reviewer reads both.
